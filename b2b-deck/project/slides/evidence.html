SPEAKER 2 — Khushi Srivastava (about 2 minutes). Thanks. Two things to hold on to: blue is always what the system said, amber is what the manager did. And the cards run left to right from “followed the system” to “overrode it”; the little wedge on each card shows how far the manager moved. Six episodes, six sectors. Let me go deep on two, because they sit at opposite ends. First, E6, top-left. A maintenance head got an IoT alert to replace a part early. Personally, they thought the part had life left. They replaced it anyway, because if the line stops, “the system flagged it” is the safest defence available. No gap at all. Now E2, pharma sourcing. The e-sourcing scorecard ranked the bids and said: give the whole order to the lowest bidder. The sourcing head didn’t reject the system. The low bidder still got the larger share. But the incumbent kept thirty percent. Why? The trigger was a price-driven tender, but the constraint was regulation: if a new supplier’s material fails an audit, a plant can stop. The saving was real, but nobody gets blamed for a split award. Not human versus machine: a negotiated middle. Across all six, only one is a clean follow and none is a clean ignore. Mostly, managers bargain with the system. They split, they escalate, they follow it on normal days and drop it in the festive peak, they read three numbers out of dozens.
