05 · Researcher’s note

What the field taught us about studying judgment.

What was hard

  • Managers first narrated the official process; the real episode surfaced only after repeated “why?” probes.
  • Confidentiality: we rarely saw the actual screens, so we relied on recall.
  • Every story was told after the outcome was known — a hindsight risk.

What worked

  • Asking “tell us about the last time you disagreed with the system” instead of opinions on AI.
  • One six-part episode template kept every conversation on decisions, not views.

Next time

  • Interview two actors on the same decision to cross-check accounts.
  • Seek overrides that went wrong — our sample likely favours success stories.
  • Observe one live decision instead of reconstructing it.

05 Reflection

SPEAKER 5 — [Name 5] (about 2 minutes). Thank you. We want to be honest about how we got these stories, because the method shaped what we found. The hardest part was getting past the official version. Almost every manager first described the process as it is written: the scorecard, the approval matrix. The real episode only came out after the second or third “why”. We also rarely saw the actual screens; confidentiality meant we relied on memory, and every story was told after the outcome was known, which invites hindsight. What worked was one change in our question. When we asked “What do you think of AI in decisions?”, we got opinions. When we asked “Tell us about the last time you disagreed with the system”, we got decisions. And using the same six-part template for every episode kept conversations on facts rather than views. If we did this again, we’d do three things. Interview two people on the same decision, say the manager and their boss, to cross-check accounts. Actively look for overrides that went wrong, because our sample probably over-represents success stories; nobody volunteers their bad calls. And observe one decision live, rather than reconstruct it. So, to bring it together: across six episodes, technology rarely made the B2B decision. What it did was shape what managers could defend. Marketers who understand that difference will sell to the judgment, not just to the algorithm. Thank you, we’re happy to take questions.
