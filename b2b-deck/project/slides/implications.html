Implications

SPEAKER 4 — Lavanya Krishan Sharma (about 2 minutes). Thanks. If decisions really run on these four logics, then a lot of B2B marketing today, which pitches tools on accuracy and features, is talking to the wrong part of the buyer. The real customer is the judgment behind the dashboard. Here’s what each logic implies, and each one traces back to an episode. Shield: buyers follow tools that protect them. So if you sell analytics or AI into a regulated buyer, “ninety-something percent accurate” is weaker than “every decision leaves an audit trail you can show your boss”. Sell defensibility. Override: in E1 the account manager saw a churn risk the CRM missed. For a selling firm, that means relationship signals and field notes are data, not anecdotes. And if you build tools, give users an override that captures the reason, because that’s how the model learns what it can’t see. Argue: in E3 the dashboard didn’t decide anything; it was ammunition in an internal negotiation. Most B2B purchases have a champion who has to win an internal sale. Give that champion the evidence pack: the numbers finance and procurement will ask for. Filter: in E5 the sales ops lead watched three metrics out of dozens. Content that doesn’t connect to the few numbers the buyer’s boss reviews is effectively invisible. Find those numbers first. Notice that none of these says “replace judgment with technology”. Each one is about designing offers, content and sales conversations around how judgment actually uses technology.
