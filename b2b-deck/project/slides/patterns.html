SPEAKER 3 — Chaitanya Sharma (about 2 minutes). Thank you. When we laid the six episodes side by side, two questions explained almost every choice. First, the vertical axis: if this goes wrong, who carries the blame? Second, the horizontal: does the manager hold context the model simply cannot see — a relationship, a season, a new person at the client? Put them together and you get four logics, and each dot is one of our episodes. Top-left, Shield: the model’s view is good enough and the manager’s neck is on the line, so they follow, because a system-backed decision is defensible. That’s our maintenance head. Bottom-right, Override: rich private knowledge, lower personal exposure, so experience wins. That’s the sales manager who knew about the client’s new buyer. Top-right is the most interesting one, Argue: high stakes and private knowledge. Here managers neither obey nor ignore; they use the system’s data as evidence to push a decision upward, like the deeper discount or the split award. Bottom-left, Filter: routine calls, where attention simply goes wherever the boss looks. Three things cut across this. One, trust is situational: look at the dashed arc. The same logistics manager followed the optimiser for most of the year and overrode it in the festive season. Two, sector sets the default: regulated settings lean towards Shield, relationship-heavy ones towards Override and Argue. Three, our central insight: managers decide fast, on experience — System 1 in Kahneman’s terms — and call on the tool’s slow, deliberate analysis, System 2, mainly to justify and defend. The tool is System 2 on demand. So what does that mean for people who sell to these managers? Lavanya. [Click]
